What to check Before Booking Consolidator Tickets

To ensure a smooth journey, verify these six essential details before confirming any premium flight.

  • All-Inclusive Total Price

    Ensure your final quote includes all government taxes, carrier surcharges, and issuance fees with zero unexpected checkout add-ons.

  • Checked Baggage Allowances

    Confirm the baggage allowance for your itinerary, including the number of checked bags and weight limits. These can vary by airline, route, and fare.

  • Cabin Class per Segment

    Check connecting flights carefully. Verify that short regional feeder legs are booked in premium cabins and not downgraded to basic economy.

  • Airport Lounge Privileges

    Check which premium airport benefits come with your ticket, including lounge access, priority boarding, and fast-track security where available.

  • Change & Cancellation Terms

    Review ticket flexibility in advance. Understand carrier change penalties, cancellation fees, and travel credit validity rules before payment.

  • Frequent Flyer Mileage Accrual

    Check whether your ticket’s booking class qualifies for frequent flyer miles and elite status credits. Earning rates can vary by airline, fare type, and loyalty program.

Frequently Asked Questions About Business Class Consolidator Fares

Our access to wholesale airline fares means we can often offer the same Business Class flight for less than the airline’s published price. Savings depend on your route and travel dates. When comparing offers, check the total price, including taxes and fees, along with each ticket’s change and refund rules.

You get a dedicated travel advisor who can compare unpublished fares across several airlines. If you’re flying into London and home from Paris, for example, we can price the whole trip, confirm the cabin on each flight, and help you rebook if your plans change.

Enter your airline booking reference and last name on the airline’s website. Check your travel dates and the cabin on every flight, including connections. Once your ticket is issued, you should also receive a 13-digit e-ticket number for each traveler. If yours is missing, contact us and we’ll confirm whether the airline has issued your ticket.

Many business class consolidator tickets earn frequent flyer miles. How many you earn depends on your loyalty program, airline and booking class, usually shown as a single letter. Ask us for that code before you book so you can check your program’s earning for your itinerary.

Contact your travel advisor before departure as soon as you know your plans have changed. If your fare allows a date change, we’ll quote the full cost, including any fees and fare difference. If you need to cancel, we’ll check whether your ticket qualifies for a refund or travel credit.

Ask each agency for a written quote showing the total price, the cabin on every flight, and the cancellation terms. If a broker claims IATA accreditation, ask for its agency code and verify it with IATA. Avoid sellers who insist on a wire transfer or pressure you to pay before sharing the fare rules.
